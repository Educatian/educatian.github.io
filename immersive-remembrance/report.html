Working draft, not a final deliverable. Version 0.1, September 28, 2026. It is written from the funded proposal and the team's early 3D archive work, before framework development begins. It has not yet been reviewed by the co-PIs, the Alabama Veterans Museum & Archives or the advisory cohort. The final report is due at the end of Year 1 (Months 10–12). Specifications marked proposed are team recommendations still to be decided.

1. Purpose & scope

This report describes the open architecture of the Living Archive VR Framework, the primary software product of the IMLS project Immersive Remembrance: AI-Driven Archival Retrieval for VR Learning (MG-260576-OMS-26). It has three audiences:

  • The project team: a shared specification for building the retrieval pipeline (Hu), the VR environment (Kim) and the study instrumentation (Moon).
  • Museum partners and the advisory cohort: a reviewable account of what the system does with archival material, so curators can check that it protects their authority over the narrative.
  • Adopting institutions: the basis for the open-source release and the "Zero-to-Hero" adoption manual.

In scope: the archival ingestion workflow, the corpus data model, the runtime retrieval engine, the VR client, the on-premises deployment, telemetry, and the quality criteria the system must meet before visitors use it. Out of scope: the psychometric instruments and the statistical analysis plan, which are covered in the study protocol.

2. Design principles

Every design decision in this report is tested against seven principles. The first is non-negotiable.

P1Verbatim only. Every word a visitor hears or reads comes from an approved archival source or from text a curator wrote. The runtime system selects content; it never generates or paraphrases.
P2Curatorial authority. Nothing reaches a visitor without a named curator's approval, and a curator can withdraw any item at any time.
P3Data stays at the museum. At runtime the corpus lives on hardware on museum premises, and the gallery system needs no internet connection.
P4Low-code for staff. Museum staff can ingest, tag, approve and publish material without writing code.
P5Open and portable. Another museum can deploy the framework by changing its data and configuration, never its source code. It uses open formats and no single-vendor dependency.
P6Accessible by design. Captions, adjustable text and seated use are built in, and a non-VR path offers the same content.
P7Measurable. The system produces the telemetry the research questions need, without collecting personal information.

3. System overview

The framework has two halves that meet only through an approved, versioned corpus bundle. The authoring side is used by museum staff and the research team to digitize, segment, tag and approve archival material. The gallery side runs the visitor experience on standalone headsets and an on-premises edge node.

A visitor interaction runs as follows. The visitor picks up or inspects a 3D artifact. The client builds an interaction context (artifact, hotspot, dwell, what has already played) and sends it to the edge node on the gallery network. The retrieval API filters the corpus to approved nodes linked to that artifact, ranks them, and returns the identifier and verbatim content of the best one. The client then plays the original audio segment (or shows the text) with captions, and logs each step as telemetry.

4. Archive & ingestion

The integrity of the corpus decides the historical fidelity of every visitor interaction, so ingestion is the most consequential part of the framework. It follows four stages from the funded work plan.

StageMonthsWhat happensOutput
1 · Rights & condition1–3Museum staff assess each candidate item for physical condition, provenance and rights status. Items involving living people or next-of-kin with unresolved consent are held back.Digitization priority list; rights status report
2 · Digitization2–5Paper is scanned and analog audio digitized to the FADGI three-star standard. Files receive persistent identifiers in the museum's collections system.Master files with provenance metadata
3 · Structuring4–7Transcripts are segmented into narrative nodes, each a coherent unit tied to a person, place, event or artifact. Curators and the team tag nodes with a shared controlled vocabulary (human in the loop).Machine-readable corpus with verified metadata
4 · Indexing6–9Approved nodes are embedded and loaded into the encrypted local vector index along with their metadata.Signed corpus bundle v1

proposed Approval is a state, not an event. Each node carries an approval state (draft → reviewed → approved → withdrawn) with the approver and date. Only approved nodes with cleared rights are compiled into a bundle. Withdrawing a node and republishing the bundle removes it from the gallery. No retraining or code change is needed.

proposed Segmentation keeps timecodes. Where an original recording exists, each node stores in and out timecodes, so the visitor hears the veteran's own voice rather than a reading of the transcript.

5. Data model

The narrative node is the unit of retrieval. Its fields map to Dublin Core so the corpus can be exchanged with collection systems. The schema below is the proposed starting point for Summit 1.

The artifact registry links each 3D model (GLB) to an artifact_id, its catalog record, named hotspots (for example the lock of a musket, or the inscription on a medal), and default vocabulary terms. The registry is what lets retrieval work without free-text queries.

6. Retrieval engine

The engine uses Retrieval-Augmented Generation (Lewis et al., 2020) without the generation step. It chooses which approved node to present and returns that node exactly as stored.

  1. Hard filter. Keep only nodes that are approved, rights-cleared and in the current bundle, and that are linked to the active artifact or hotspot. This step is a database constraint, not a model judgment, so an unrelated or unapproved node can never be returned.
  2. Semantic ranking. Rank the remaining nodes by similarity between the interaction context (artifact and hotspot terms plus the themes already heard) and each node's embedding.
  3. Session rules. Do not repeat a node within a session, favor thematic variety, and respect content-note settings.
  4. Register choice. Per the design grounded in CAMIL, short or scattered dwell favors a curator-written overview node, and sustained focus on an artifact favors a first_person node.
  5. Safe fallback. If nothing passes a relevance threshold, return the artifact's curator-written default node. The engine never returns nothing and never improvises.

The funded target is a response in 500 ms or less, from interaction to the start of narration. The proposed budget:

StepBudget (p95)
Context build on headset20 ms
Gallery network round trip40 ms
Hard filter + vector search on edge node60 ms
Session rules and register choice20 ms
Audio segment ready to play (pre-cached per artifact)150 ms
Total, with margin for Beta tuning≈ 290 ms of 500 ms

Because the corpus for a single artifact is small, candidate nodes for an artifact can be prefetched to the headset when the visitor approaches it. That makes the latency target robust to network hiccups.

7. VR client

The visitor application is built in Unity (C#) for standalone Meta Quest 3 headsets through the OpenXR toolchain, so no tethered PC is needed. It is organized as replaceable modules:

Artifact interaction

Grab, rotate and inspect GLB artifacts; hotspots raise events.

Context builder

Turns interaction events and dwell into a retrieval request.

Retrieval client

Talks to the edge API, prefetches per artifact, and handles timeouts with the fallback node.

Narration player

Spatial audio of the original recording, synchronized captions, and embedded text at adjustable size.

Telemetry emitter

Buffers events and sends them to the edge sink (see §9).

Session & comfort

Docent reset between visitors, seated mode, comfort vignette and an always-visible exit.

8. Edge node & network

  • On premises. A compact, gallery-mounted computer hosts the metadata store, vector index, retrieval API and telemetry sink. The veteran testimony corpus is never served from a commercial cloud at runtime.
  • Isolated network. Headsets and the edge node share a dedicated, encrypted gallery network with no route to the internet during operation.
  • Encrypted at rest. Corpus and telemetry storage are encrypted. Physical kiosks use cable locks and mounts.
  • Offline updates. New corpus bundles and app builds are installed from signed packages, and the node checks the signature before loading.
  • Footprint. Six kiosk stations in under 100 square feet of existing gallery space, with no structural changes.

9. Telemetry

Telemetry serves RQ2 (which interaction sequences predict engagement) and system QA (latency, failures). Events are written as JSON Lines under a pseudonymous session ID. The proposed core event set:

EventKey fields
session_start / session_endsession_id, condition, kiosk_id, build, bundle_version
artifact_grab / artifact_releaseartifact_id, t
hotspot_dwellartifact_id, hotspot, dwell_ms, gaze_source
retrieval_request / retrieval_resultcontext terms, node_id, rank, score, latency_ms, fallback
narration_start / narration_endnode_id, played_ms, completed, skipped

Telemetry contains no names, images or audio of visitors. It is linked to survey responses only through a study code issued at consent, and the linking key stays with the research team and never goes into the released dataset.

10. Security, privacy & rights

  • Rights first. Material with unclear or restricted rights is excluded from both the gallery bundle and the public release. Another museum substitutes its own rights-cleared material.
  • Sensitive details. Following the practice already used in the 3D archive, home addresses, identification-tag inscriptions and similar personal details are redacted before display. Memorial material is shown only with family permission.
  • Human subjects. Visitor recruitment and data collection run only under The University of Alabama IRB approval. School groups require documented parental consent.
  • Least data. No data is collected from visitors who decline, and participants' data is de-identified before release.
  • Master files during processing. Master files held during digitization and NLP processing (Months 1–7) are kept in encrypted, access-controlled storage, then moved to the local node.

11. Accessibility & comfort

  • The interface targets WCAG 2.1 AA where it applies to immersive environments: captions for all audio, adjustable text size, sufficient contrast and no time-limited interactions.
  • Seated use and one-handed interaction, with comfort settings on by default and simulation-sickness screening in Beta QA.
  • proposed A non-VR path: the same approved nodes, reachable on a screen or phone from the web 3D archive, for visitors who cannot or choose not to wear a headset.
  • The adoption manual is published as PDF/UA with an editable Markdown source.

12. Portability

Portability is what makes this a national-leadership product rather than a single exhibit. A new institution changes data and configuration only:

A museum suppliesThe framework provides
Its own rights-cleared transcripts and recordingsIngestion templates and a no-code tagging and approval tool
Its controlled vocabulary (or adopts ours)Vocabulary import and Dublin Core mapping
3D models of its artifacts (GLB), with hotspotsAn artifact registry editor and a GLB import path
Gallery and kiosk settingsA JSON configuration: kiosks, languages, content notes, comfort defaults

At each advisory cohort summit, member museums run the ingestion pipeline on samples of their own, very different collections. The acceptance test is that their material runs with no source-code changes.

14. Evidence from early work

The team has already published seven interactive 3D records from the AVMA galleries (3D Archive). That work tests several parts of this architecture ahead of time:

  • Artifact assets. Photo-derived GLB models with measured colors and textures cut from gallery photographs load on phones at about 1–8 MB per object. These can become the first entries in the artifact registry.
  • Paradata. Each record separates photographed, referenced and inferred parts, and labels uncertain identifications as provisional. The same honesty rule applies to narrative nodes.
  • Hotspots. Articulated mechanisms (a flintlock lock, a traverse-and-elevation mechanism, a washer engine) show that artifact parts are natural retrieval triggers.
  • Restrictive runtimes. Delivering inside a locked-down web sandbox showed that compressed-mesh decoders and blob URLs can fail silently. We now test every build under the strictest target policy. The same discipline will apply to the headset build.
  • Privacy practice. The Gold Star memorial record established the redaction and family-permission practice described in §10.

15. Open design questions

These are for the team and for Advisory Cohort Summit 1. Each one affects the principles above.

Q1 · Where are embeddings computed?

The plan budgets a commercial text-embedding API, but P3 promises that testimony stays with the museum. Recommendation: use an open-weight embedding model on UA or edge hardware, or limit any external call to rights-cleared text under a no-retention agreement.

Q2 · Is there any generative model at runtime?

The proposal describes a zero-temperature RAG. Recommendation: no generative step at all, as in §6. Retrieval-only is simpler, faster and makes P1 testable.

Q3 · What counts as "gaze"?

Quest 3 has no eye tracking. Recommendation: define dwell as head-gaze and hand-held inspection, and record gaze_source. If true eye tracking matters for RQ2, budget a different headset for a subset.

Q4 · Voice when no recording exists.

A synthetic voice reading a veteran's words raises ethical questions. Recommendation: use the original audio where it exists. Otherwise show text with an optional clearly labeled neutral narrator, never a cloned voice.

Q5 · Who writes overview nodes?

The low-load path needs short orientation texts. Recommendation: curators write and approve them as nodes, so the "summaries" never come from a model.

Q6 · Registry granularity.

Is the retrieval trigger the whole artifact or its hotspots, and who defines hotspots: curators or the 3D team?
