Research

Does hearing their words change what visitors understand?

A quasi-experimental museum study, with behavioral telemetry and interviews, designed to find out what AI-retrieved testimony in VR does and how visitors use it.

Funded by
Research questions

Two questions guide the study

RQ1 · AFFECTIVE IMPACT

Does AI-retrieved veteran testimony in VR increase historical empathy and factual retention?

Compared with traditional static exhibition media.

RQ2 · BEHAVIORAL MODELING

Which interaction sequences best predict cognitive and emotional engagement?

For example, time spent gazing at personal artifacts versus technical components.

Study design

How we will find out

PARTICIPANTS

≈1,200 visitors

Recruited at AVMA by intercept sampling over a nine-month data collection window, from school groups to military personnel.

CONDITIONS

AI-VR vs. static

Stratified, blocked random assignment to the Living Archive VR experience or a traditional static exhibit.

MEASURES

Empathy, retention, behavior

Historical Empathy Scale, factual retention items, in-VR telemetry logs and semi-structured interviews.

ANALYSIS

Mixed methods

Mixed-design ANCOVA with effect sizes (RQ1); process mining and multilevel regression (RQ2); thematic coding of interviews.

Visitor recruitment and data collection begin only under the appropriate approval from The University of Alabama Institutional Review Board.
