The challenge

Stories at risk of becoming placards

Fewer than 45,000 World War II veterans are still living. Their testimony survives in museum archives as long transcripts that few visitors will ever read.

Immersive media can deepen historical empathy, but custom VR exhibits cost more than most of the nation's 33,000+ museums can spend, and generative AI raises a real fear of invented history. We are building a framework that is dynamic, affordable and bound to each museum's own verified collection.

The Living Archive VR Framework

AI as a librarian, not a storyteller

Retrieval-Augmented Generation is used only to route. Every passage a visitor hears is taken verbatim from the museum's own archive, so curators stay in control of the story.

Curate

Museum staff and the team digitize oral histories and tag them with controlled-vocabulary metadata.

Interact

A visitor in a wireless headset handles a 3D artifact such as a medal, a uniform or a map.

Retrieve

The engine reads the interaction context and searches an index of the museum's own transcripts.

Listen

The most relevant authentic passage plays as spatial audio, with text placed in the scene.

Project at a glance

Three objectives, three years

01 · BUILD

Technical development

Engineer an open-source RAG + VR framework that retrieves authenticated archival content in real time.

02 · STUDY

Empirical validation

Test its effect on historical empathy and retention against static exhibits in a visitor study of about 1,200 people.

03 · SHARE

Field-wide dissemination

Release the code, an adoption manual and the de-identified data so any museum can reuse the work.
